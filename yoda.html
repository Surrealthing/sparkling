What is the Year of Discovery?

A 3-year journey designed for self-knowledge and direction.

Not a gap year. A purposeful, structured programme that takes teenagers from first questions to first projects to fully-formed life choices.

Year 1

Explore & Self-Discover

Students start by asking who they are and what matters to them. Through guided exploration, mentorship and exposure to diverse fields, they surface real interests — not imagined ones.

Year 2

Deepen & Build

With a direction emerging, students go deeper. Real research, real prototypes, real entrepreneurship. AS Level coursework anchors rigor while projects carry the learning.

Year 3

Showcase & Launch

A body of work comes together — A Levels, capstone projects, a real portfolio. Students leave with a clear sense of what they're building, and where they're headed next.

The Four-Part Framework

Four threads, woven across three years.

Every skill, every project and every conversation is held inside one of these four structures. Together they build a whole human, not just a candidate.

01

Purpose

Students work on self-awareness and identity — who they are, what they value, and what their deepest contribution might be. Purpose is the foundation; everything else grows from it.

02

Self-Learning & Research

Students learn how to learn. They ask their own questions, read widely, run experiments and build the habits that make them independent thinkers for life.

03

Entrepreneurship

Students learn to see problems as invitations — and move from idea to prototype to small-scale launch. The goal is agency: the belief that they can build what the world needs.

04

Showcase

Students learn to show their work and their thinking — on stage, in portfolios, to mentors and to the world. By the end, they own a story only they could tell.

Real Stories, Real Impact

What the YODA journey actually looks like.

From uncertainty to clarity

Samriddhi's Journey

Samriddhi entered the Year of Discovery unsure about what she wanted to pursue. Through guided exploration, research, and hands-on projects, she discovered her interest in policy and public systems.

By the end of the program, she had:

Built a strong portfolio of meaningful work
Gained confidence in expressing her ideas
Secured admission into a top liberal arts programme

Turning passion into direction

Mihika's Journey

Mihika used the Year of Discovery to explore her passion for design and storytelling. Through entrepreneurship projects and self-learning, she transformed her interests into real-world experiences.

Her journey helped her:

Develop strong communication and critical thinking skills
Participate in national-level design and writing competitions
Build a unique profile that stood out in college applications
